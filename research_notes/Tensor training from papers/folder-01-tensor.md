# Folder 01 maps on weight tensors

A purely tensor step reads an array of weights and writes an array of weights. It has no loss, no labels, no token ids, no calibration text, and no backward pass. The sentence below uses that test on the Proof environments in folder 01. Existence statements, operator norms, and eigenvalue lists are recorded as facts about a map, and they are not themselves writes. Unproved items named in the assignment (Riemannian and Regge positive mass, Ricci classification, a mesh-free Lichnerowicz bound, cosmic censorship, Kontsevich–Zagier, motivic coactions) are not proposed as steps. Where a later synthesis asserts a step the Proof environments do not construct, the Proof environment is the text that counts.

Primary sources are the two 23 October 2026 TeX papers, the 6 October heap and Dijkstra notes, the 30 October synthesis, and the 31 October cubical-projection note. The binding referee compression is the folder-01 note and the three-folder report. Code under `hodgemix-go` is read as an existing implementation, not as a new theorem.

## For each proved object, what array goes in, what array comes out, and what the tensor must satisfy

### Takeaway
Eight proved objects are closed-form array maps under a stated hypothesis, and several of the numbers attached to them are norms of those maps rather than further maps. Edge duality returns the same cochain. The coboundary, the nested-cycle projections, the integer-ratio hat, the box, the cycle Laplacian, and the cap write a different array or a different length. The algebraic Schöberl correction is a map only after a chain map \(Q\) is supplied. The cubical projection of 31 October is a map on a uniform tensor grid, and its constant \(2^d\) is the bound on that map. Heap insert, decrease-key, and extract-min rewrite a queue of integer keys, not a weight array.

- Edge duality, Lemma l:dual. Input: a 0-cochain, one real number per vertex, or a 1-cochain, one real number per oriented edge, of a finite simplicial complex. The identity is \(\int_e\phi_{e'}=\delta_{ee'}\) and \(\lambda_i(v_j)=\delta_{ij}\), hence \(R\circ W=\mathrm{id}\) on 0-cochains and on 1-cochains. ### Inferences
- The hypothesis “uniform grid” attaches to Theorem U and Theorem U′ only. The hypothesis “product of cycles, factor length at least 3” attaches to the torus gap formula. The hypothesis “degree 0 or 1” attaches to the proved Whitney identities, the nested-cycle block, and the hat as a 0-cochain map with a 1-form partner. The hypothesis “radix at least 3” is the gap minimum’s restriction \(r_j\ge 3\); it is not a hypothesis of the hat, the cap, the cubical bound, or the heap, whose radices satisfy \(r_i\ge 2\).
- A map preserves a tensor identity when the cited equation says the output equals the input or equals another composition: \(R\circ W=\mathrm{id}\), \(R_k P_k=\mathrm{id}\), \(\pi\) on \(V_h\), \(A_{\mathrm{hat}}\) and \(A_{\mathrm{box}}\) when the two axes have the same length, and the cap off \(S\). The same maps change values in the complementary case: \(\delta\), \(\Pi_0\) off its image, \(\Pi_1\) on unequal halves, \(A_{\mathrm{hat}}\) at integer \(m>1\), \(A_{\mathrm{box}}\) when a target cell covers more than one source cell, \(L\) off an eigenspace, the cap on \(S\), and \(\pi_k\) on a form that is not already in \(V^k_h\).
- The code functions `dCycle`, `prolong0`, `prolong1`, `restrict0`, `restrict1`, and `l1Cycle` are the arrays in Definition d:nested. Composing them gives \(\Pi_0\) and \(\Pi_1\). `math.go`’s `project` and `splineGrid` implement the bump-mollified axis map, whose \(S_v\) invertibility is an assumption and whose measured inverse reaches \(62810.123\). That function is not the box kernel \(\varphi_h=h^{-1}1_{[-h/2,h/2]}\). `Spectrum.Hodge` partitions a discrete Fourier transform of a grid; the proved symbol it can cite is \(\lambda_k=\sum_j(2-2\cos(2\pi k_j/r_j))\), not an orthogonal decomposition with a Proof environment. — [whitney.go](file:///C:/Users/grego/hodgemix-go/whitney.go); [math.go](file:///C:/Users/grego/hodgemix-go/math.go)

### Gaps
- No Proof environment writes the higher-degree simplicial duality as an array map. Using it as a tensor step would use Whitney’s 1957 theorem, which this folder cites and does not prove.
- The small-data Euler update \(\hat h\mapsto(1-\Delta t\,\lambda_k)\hat h+\Delta t\,\widehat Q(h)\) is a formula in the text of Theorem t:decay, and it is conditional on \(\|Q(h)\|\le C_Q\|h\|^2\) for \(\|h\|\le 1\), on \(0<\Delta t\le 1/\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}\), and on \(\|h^0\|\le\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}}/(2C_Q)\). \(C_Q\) is not computed. The proved contraction factor is \(\rho=(1+\rho_1)/2\) with \(\rho_1=1-\Delta t\,\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}}\), so \(\rho=1-(\Delta t\,\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}})/2\). Both TeX abstracts state the faster factor \(1-\Delta t\,\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}}\). This note does not treat that update as an unconditional tensor step.
- Theorem t:hatnorm does not give a closed row formula for a non-integer ratio. The \(3{:}2\) and \(5{:}4\) rows are measurements.

## Which objects are maps a trainer can call, and which only state existence or stability

### Takeaway
A trainer can call the nested-cycle operators, the integer-ratio hat, the box, the cycle Laplacian, the cap once \(S\) and \(B\) are already arrays, and the uniform-grid cubical projection. The numbers \(\sqrt{2}\), \(1\), \(\sqrt{m/2}\), \(\sqrt{m}\), \(1\) for the box, \(2-2\cos(2\pi k/r)\), and \(2^d\) do not update a weight. The algebraic lemma, the Betti statement, the decay theorem, and the heap complexity displays do not update a weight either.

### Cited Findings
- Callable closed-form maps, each with the hypothesis in the previous section. `whitney.go` already contains the nested-cycle six: `dCycle`, `prolong0`, `prolong1`, `restrict0`, `restrict1`, `l1Cycle`. The hat row at integer \(m\) is two entries \(\tfrac12\) or one entry \(1\), as in Theorem t:hatnorm, and the box is cell averaging with \(\|A_{\mathrm{box}}\|_{\mathrm{RMS}}=1\).

# Multi-Radix Whitney Theory: referee notes on folder 01

Seven files, six distinct documents. None was reviewed from its filename. PDF text was extracted with pypdf; both `.tex` sources were read in full. The two 23 October 2026 TeX papers share a proved core and are not the same paper. The Google Docs FEEC survey is not a rendering of either TeX file.

Printed bylines and PDF metadata disagree on the author string. Metadata is reported only where the body has no author.

## Identity, claim, and which files are the same paper

### Takeaway
The folder holds six documents by overlapping names (Gregory Disney-Leugers, Gregory Disney, an unbylined synthesis, an unbylined Google Docs survey, and a PDF whose metadata author is “Greg (revised with Claude)”): two dated mathematical notes that share a proof core, a 30 October synthesis that sketches that core and leaves the \(L^2\) bound open, a 31 October note that supplies an explicit \(2^d\) bound for uniform tensor grids, a 6 October multi-radix heap, and a Dijkstra instantiation of that heap. The survey is a literature essay, not a theorem paper.

## Mathematical objects

### Takeaway
The geometric papers use Whitney forms on a finite simplicial complex, the Sobolev de Rham complex, an algebraic commuting projection built from an invertible chain map, the cycle graph and its product (the digit torus), a triangulated disk, and a Cartesian grid. The 31 October note uses lowest-order cubical Whitney forms on a uniform tensor grid. The heap papers use a mixed-radix bucket queue, not a de Rham complex.

## Relation to classical FEEC, Whitney forms, and bounded commuting projections

### Takeaway
What the folder itself marks as standard is the Whitney form, the trimmed polynomial complex, the unboundedness of \(\mathcal{I}_h\), and the Schöberl idempotent correction. What it marks as proved here, beyond that citation, is the degree-0 and degree-1 duality and coboundary calculation, the exact nested-cycle norms \(\sqrt{2}\) and \(1\), the exact hat RMS norm, the disk Gauss–Bonnet mass, the grid divergence identity, a conditional small-data Euler decay, a cellwise cap, an \(S_2\) holonomy counterexample, and a box-smoother projection on uniform tensor grids with constant \(2^d\). Christiansen–Rapetti is not named in any file.

### Inferences
- Against Arnold–Falk–Winther, the folder does not prove a new stability theorem for the simplicial families \(\mathcal{P}_r\Lambda^k\) and \(\mathcal{P}_r^-\Lambda^k\). It uses their complex as the language, proves the cohomological injection for a chain projection, and, on 31 October, proves a concrete \(L^2\) bound for lowest-order cubical product elements on uniform, possibly anisotropic, tensor grids. Spectral convergence is then invoked from AFW.
- Against Schöberl and Christiansen–Winther, the algebraic lemma \(\pi=Q Q_h^{-1}\) is a written proof of a standard correction. The analytic existence proof is not redone. The box kernel and the symbol \((3+\cos\theta)/4\) are the part the 31 October note calls new, and they are restricted to translation-invariant uniform grids.
- The hat-norm theorem is the canonical interpolant’s \(L^2\) unboundedness, made exact for an integer downsampling of one axis: the operator commutes with \(\delta\) and is the identity on equal axes, and its RMS norm grows like \(\sqrt{m/2}\) or \(\sqrt{m}\). That is a restatement of the reason a smoothed projection is used, with a constant the classical citations in this folder do not compute.
- t:cap does not advance cosmic censorship. t:pmt2 and t:lin do not advance the Riemannian or Regge positive-mass theorems. The papers that say this are the TeX files; MRWT’s abstract agrees, while MRWT Theorem 6.2’s “large sphere” wording is looser than t:lin.

### Gaps

- The 31 October claim of anisotropy-independent stability is for a tensor product of uniform 1D meshes. It does not address a general simplicial mesh with a bad shape-regularity constant, which is the setting of the Schöberl constant the TeX papers refuse to re-estimate.
- Classical papers named in the bibliographies were not re-read for this note. Attributions of what is “standard” are the folder’s own sentences, checked against the proofs that are or are not written in the folder.

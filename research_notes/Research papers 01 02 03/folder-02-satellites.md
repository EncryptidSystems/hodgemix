Numerical values that the papers actually print are the Kuhn convergence tables, the BDF multiplier minima, the Lemma G floating-point scans and the three BPST ratios, the secant energy identity to ten decimals, the twistor script checks, and the RoPE sanity residuals. ### Inferences

- The Kuhn \(L^2\) rates near 3 and \(H^1\) rates near 2 for \(q=2\), and near 2 and 1 for \(q=1\), are what the theorem predicts for the spatial error when the time step is small. The paper itself says the \(k\propto h\) runs are time-error dominated and that the \(L^2\)-projection start is outside the sketched theorem. The blow-up at \(\varepsilon=0.15\), \(n=4\), \(q=1\), is one mesh, not a measured \(\varepsilon_0\).
- The two “gaps” are not the same number. Lemma G `(2)` puts the first positive Coulomb eigenvalue at 6 on unit \(S^4\), which is 0.6298 after multiplication by \(4/C_V\). The smooth-twistor note’s conditional coercivity is 4 on unit \(S^4\) in the normalisation \(2\|d^+ a\|^2\ge 2\mu\|a\|^2\) with \(\mu=2\), which is 0.41985 in V-units. A lower bound of 4 does not contradict a first eigenvalue of 6. Calling both “the sharp gap 6, verified two ways” does not match either note’s tag.
- The Ric\(=3g\) identity printed in the revised Hodge note gives, on the Coulomb slice, \(2\|d_A^+ a\|^2=\|\nabla_A a\|^2+3\|a\|^2\), hence a lower bound of 3 plus a nonnegative term. That identity alone is not the eigenvalue 6.
